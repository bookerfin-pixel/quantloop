Before settling on H1, tried three momentum-family ideas from the backlog
against `python -m bot.backtest` on the current cached data (2026-08-24 to

entry describes as losing money before costs). None cleared the backtest

- Vol regime filter (skip entries when 7d vol is above its trailing median):
  gross pnl -235 (vs H0's -300 on the same window), cost coverage -0.94.
  Fewer, marginally less bad trades, still net negative before costs.
- 168h lookback / wider (+-3%) bands: gross pnl -844, worse than H0. Bigger
  bands did not mean bigger captured moves here, just fewer chances to be
  right.
- Cross sectional top-3-of-6 by 7 day return: gross pnl -168, the least bad
  of the momentum variants, still negative.
- Vol-scaled stop (exit on a 24h adverse move > 1.5x hourly vol * sqrt(24)):
  gross pnl -276, more fills than H0 (109) and still negative.

Reading: in a falling tape, filtering *which* long entries a trend-following
signal takes does not flip the sign, because the underlying problem is
that the tape does not reward being long, not that any particular entry is
noisy. Only the mean reversion family (buying deep, multi standard deviation
dips) showed positive gross pnl on this window, which is what H1 uses.
Left as a note rather than backlog entries because it isn't a new idea, it's
what happened when trying old ones on the current data; a future agent
re-running these exact filters on this window should expect the same result,
but a different (less adverse) window could easily favour them instead.
