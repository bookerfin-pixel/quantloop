## Looking for a fourth family for challenger3

The last three runs (09-17, 09-18, 09-19) converged on: no long-only variant
of the momentum or mean-reversion signal, however filtered, clears
backtest_gate over the real trailing year, because every pair fell 46-81%
peak to trough and any signal that's net long often enough during that
period loses on cost-adjusted terms. H2 (vol_breakout, merged today) was the
first idea with a mechanism that's rare/inactive by construction during a
trending fall (a volatility squeeze) rather than just a tighter trigger on
the same signal, and it passed the gate. For challenger3 I looked for a
second, different mechanism in that same spirit — something that doesn't
just filter momentum or reversion harder. Tried three, all against real data,
all rejected:

**Equal-weight rebalancing (volatility harvesting).** Periodically rebalance
the ten-pair basket back to equal weight; even with zero directional view,
positive cross-sectional dispersion with imperfect correlation produces a
mechanical premium (~0.5 x (average variance - basket variance) annualised).
Computed directly from state/history: average pairwise return correlation
across the ten pairs is ~0.79 (trailing 90d and trailing 365d agree), giving
an annualised bonus of only ~3.7-4.8% of equity before any trading cost — and
that's before accounting for the rebalancing turnover needed to harvest it.
Worse, this basket correlation is too high for the mechanism to be flat
during a fall: a fully invested rebalanced basket still tracks the basket's
own ~53% trailing-year drawdown, since the bonus only shaves a few points off
the top. Not a real answer to what killed the other six variants.

**Volume-confirmed breakout.** New N-hour high (120h) plus trailing volume
(72h average) elevated at least 1.5x its own 30 day (720h) baseline; exit on
a close below a trailing 72h low. Simulated directly against the real
trailing 365 days with entry/exit hysteresis matching how strategies here
actually hold positions (not a fixed forward-return window). 24 trades total
across the ten pairs, average -4.36% per trade — not a near-miss, clearly
negative. A volume surge did not distinguish real breakouts from the false
starts that get chopped up during a one-directional fall; same failure mode
as the already-killed breakout/momentum variants, just with an extra
precondition that didn't help.

**Cross-sectional breadth gate.** Only trade when at least some fraction of
the ten pairs are above their own N-hour EMA (a market-wide risk-on
confirmation, as opposed to a pair's own vol/EMA regime, which was already
tried and killed per the backlog). Checked whether breadth level actually
predicts the basket's own forward 24h return over the trailing 365 days — it
does not; average forward return is indistinguishable across low, mid, and
high breadth terciles, all within noise of zero. Breadth here describes what
has already happened, not what happens next, so a gate built on it would add
no timing power the already-killed per-pair regime filters didn't already
lack.

None of these clear "why it should work" with real arithmetic, so none are
going into challenger3 today — filling the slot with something I don't
believe in would spend 60 days of test capacity to relearn what the last
three runs already showed. All three are logged in the backlog under
"ideas the evidence says not to bother with" so a future run doesn't retry
them from scratch.
