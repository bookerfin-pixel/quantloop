## Confirmed again (3rd day running): the backtest_gate slot-1 fallback bug

`gate/backtest_gate.py:changed_slot()` (protected) looks for a changed
`configs/challenger<k>.yaml` in the PR diff; if none is found — true for any
notes-only or backlog-only PR — it silently falls back to
`config.challengers()[0]`, always challenger1, and reruns *that* slot's
backtest over the full `backtest_gate.max_days` (365) window, whether or not
the PR touched it. First found 2026-09-17, reproduced again 2026-09-18, and
reproduced a third time just now:

    python -m bot.backtest --config configs/challenger1.yaml   # H1, mean_reversion, 365d
      max_drawdown: -0.4502   cost_coverage: -1.31   gross_pnl: -1773.85
    python -m bot.backtest --config configs/champion.yaml      # H0, ts_momentum, 365d
      max_drawdown: -0.7787   cost_coverage: -1.64   gross_pnl: -4256.21

Both fail `backtest_gate`'s own bounds (cost_coverage >= 1.0, max_drawdown
>= -30%) badly. challenger1 is an approved, running prospective test — it
does not need to re-pass `backtest_gate`, that gate is for a hypothesis
*entering* a slot — but the fallback backtests it anyway on every PR that
doesn't touch a challenger config, so it fails the whole PR. This has now
closed two notes-only PRs (#3, #4) for a reason that has nothing to do with
their actual content. I cannot fix this myself (`gate/backtest_gate.py` is
protected). Flagging again for Fin, same two fixes suggested on 09-18: (a)
skip the backtest step entirely when the diff touches no challenger config
and no strategy code, mirroring what `gate/check_ledger.py` already does at
its own top; or (b) when no slot changed, don't fall back to slot 1 — just
skip. Either lets a legitimate bookkeeping PR merge on its own merits
regardless of how a running test's in-sample replay looks today. Until one
of those lands (or the trailing-365-day window rolls far enough past the
crash below that challenger1's own replay clears -30%/1.0, which is not
guaranteed before H1's verdict around 2026-11-15), any PR that doesn't fill
or touch a slot is likely to keep failing this way. I'm submitting today's
PR anyway, same reasoning as 09-18: the record should be readable via `gh pr
view` even closed.

## Structural finding, corrects a line in the backlog: strategies are capped at a 720 hour (30 day) lookback, always

While looking for a hypothesis that could plausibly clear the 365 day
replay, checked whether a much longer regime filter (60-120 day) than what's
been tried (21-28 days) could work. It can't, and not for a data-availability
reason. Both the live loop and the backtest slice candles to
`risk.yaml:history_hours` (720) rows immediately before calling the strategy
function:

- `bot/run.py`: `candles = {p: df.tail(hist).reset_index(drop=True) ...}` with `hist = history_hours`.
- `bot/backtest.py`'s per-bar replay: `window = {p: compact[p].iloc[max(0, pos[p][i] - hist): pos[p][i]]}`,
  same `hist`, explicitly so (per its own docstring) "the backtest cannot be
  kinder than paper trading."

So no strategy, live or backtested, can ever see more than ~29 days of
candles per decision, regardless of how much history state/history holds
(the two year Coinbase backfill doesn't change this). The backlog's regime
filter entry says "worth another look once more candle history has
accumulated" — that's not the blocker; the 720h slice is. Corrected that
line below rather than let a future run retry something structurally
impossible as written. The longest filters already tried (500h vol median,
672h EMA, both ~09-17) are close to the practical ceiling for this system.

## Why no slot filled today

Went looking for a sixth variant, structurally different from what's been
tried, with a real shot at clearing `backtest_gate`'s hard bounds over the
actual trailing 365 days: every pair down 46-81% peak to trough over that
year (BTC -46%/-54%, the rest worse; reproduced above via the champion and
challenger1 backtests, first measured 09-17). Between 09-17 and 09-18 five
variants were tried and backtested against this same real window: a vol
regime filter, an EMA regime filter, a standalone cross-sectional top-3
ranker, a top-3 overlay on ts_momentum's own bands, and a z-score stop-loss
on mean_reversion at two settings. All five failed on cost coverage and/or
drawdown, several by a wide margin (see backlog entries below, restored from
the closed PRs). The common thread across all of them: with no shorting and
at most a 30 day lookback, every version was net long often enough during a
sustained, universe-wide one-directional fall to lose money on average, and
tightening entries or adding a stop reduced how much was lost without
flipping gross pnl positive. I don't have a mechanism today that addresses
that directly rather than just trading less within the same losing
direction, so I'm not filling challenger2 or challenger3 with something I
can't back with real conviction — that would spend 60 days of test capacity
on a bet I don't believe in, which is the same thing rule 4's "do not game
the number, change the idea" is warning against, just applied to the
backtest gate instead of the ledger field.

One thing worth flagging rather than acting on: live paper trading the last
3-4 days has been a sharp rally (champion +9.35%, basket of 10 +14.69% per
state/summary.md), not reflected in the negative 365-day backtest figure
above, which is dominated by the earlier and larger fall. That's exactly why
this system only lets the prospective window count as evidence — a filter
tuned to "the market just turned" would be fitting the most recent data
point, not a mechanism. Not treating it as a reason to force a hypothesis
through either.

## What I'd want a future run to try instead

Same conclusion the 09-18 note reached, still true: not "tune momentum or

specifically should look different from the trailing year. Absent that,
today's honest answer is: no slot filled, and the case for it is above
rather than asserted.
