# Hypothesis backlog

Ideas waiting for the challenger slot. The agent may add to this file on any
run and should pick from it when the slot is free, taking the one with the
best cost arithmetic, not the one that looks best in the backtest. Remove an
idea when it becomes a ledger entry or when a result makes it moot.

## Candidates

- Cross sectional momentum with a weekly rebalance (reopened 2026-10-05 by Fin's maintainer session; the 2026-09-17 entry under "not to bother with" ranked the pairs again every hour, which churned, and judged it on absolute profit in a falling year). Measured on daily closes of the ten pairs over five years (2021-10 to 2026-10; nine pairs until XRP's candles begin in July 2023), long only, fully invested in the top k by trailing return at equal weight, 15 bps per unit of weight traded, against the equal weight basket of all ten. With a weekly rebalance all 18 variants (lookback 3, 7, 14, 30, 60, 90 days; top 2, 3, 5) had positive skill: yearly Sharpe ratio of skill 0.06 to 1.09, average 0.48. Against their own twins (the same weights slid in time by 30 days or more: same turnover, same costs, no link to the market) 7 of the 18 beat 95% of twins, where about 1 would by luck. The 14 day lookback beat 98% for every k and the 3 day one 94% to 99.8%. Lookbacks of 30 days and more cannot be told from luck (62% to 87%). Holding the weakest instead lost in all 18. With a daily rebalance costs take it all (weight traded 38 to 325 times a year against 15 to 82 with a weekly one; average Sharpe -0.26). By year, 14 days and top 3: +1.0, +1.5, +1.5, +0.5 for 2022 to 2025 and -0.5 for 2026 to date, so this year is against it. Caveats: in sample; 18 variants that overlap; daily bars, not the hourly engine. In the engine: max_weight is 0.25, and it must rebalance at one fixed hour a week and otherwise return current_weights, or the hourly loop will rank the pairs again every hour and churn as the September attempt did. Size matters more than the lookback. A rough replay of the 18 variants through rule 1 as it stands from ruleset 7 (five years, a window a week; rough because it has no champion, no drawdown guard and no fill count in it, and reads "finished trades made money" as the book being up after costs): a fully invested book is ended early in 51% of 120 day windows, because the few coins it holds lose more than 15% and more than the basket; a book that holds half its equity (for instance the top 2 at a quarter each) is ended early in 1%. At half the book, in sample: killed at day 60 in 34% of windows, killed at day 120 in 11%, unproven in 34%, promoted in 20% (5 of those 20 by a fast pass, which the real rule would not give a test that is short of 30 fills at day 60; see below). So run it at half the book, and say in the entry that a kill or `unproven` is likelier than a promotion even if the effect is real (expected daily skill t after 120 days on the in sample figure: 0.3 to 0.6). What it tells us that the running tests do not: this chooses between coins and holds its exposure near constant, so it makes about 50 picks a year per name held where the timing strategies make a handful of in or out calls, and its skill does not turn on which way the market went. Fills: a weekly swap of one or two names is 2 to 4 fills a week, 17 to 34 in 60 days, so it may come up short of 30 at the first look; it then turns on whether its finished trades made money (every weekly swap finishes one).
- Longer horizon: 168 hour lookback with wider bands (entry +3%, exit −3%). Fewer, bigger moves; the cost per unit of captured move falls roughly in proportion. Tried against the current data (2026-09-16): backtest cost coverage was worse than H0's, not better (-4.75 vs -0.96) — wider bands wait longer to exit, which let losses run further in this falling window. The "fewer, bigger moves" logic held for entries but not for the matching exit; a version of this idea should pair the wider entry band with a faster exit, not the same one.
- Weekend throttle: no new entries from Friday 20:00 UTC to Monday 00:00 UTC. Liquidity thins and spreads widen at the weekend, so the modelled 5 bps slippage understates the true cost of those fills.
- Volatility targeted exit: exit when a position's trailing 24h move against it exceeds 1.5 times its hourly vol times sqrt(24). A stop expressed in vol units rather than a fixed return, so it adapts per pair. Tried as an add-on to ts_momentum's exit against the current data (2026-09-16): still failed the backtest cost coverage gate (-0.89, vs H0's own -0.96), a small improvement but not enough. Worth retrying once a challenger has actually run so there is live decisions.csv evidence of how ts_momentum's exit lags, rather than backtest-only.

## Ideas the evidence says not to bother with

The fast signal is the problem, not the missing short side. At the slow 720h horizon the short leg made +12% net in the falling year and lost 38% in the rising one; over the two years, long only did better (+58% against +40% with both legs, same screen). Access is the other wall: licensed crypto perpetuals in Australia are wholesale only (Coinbase Australia from Aug 2026, Kraken, Independent Reserve), and the retail route is CFDs capped at 2:1 leverage under ASIC's order (expires 23 May 2027, under review), with short financing of 4-8% a year at IG Australia. Note (2026-09-21, Fin): the entries below dated 2026-09-16 to 2026-09-19 quote
the gate as it was then (cost coverage >= 1.0, max drawdown >= -30% absolute,
in sample profit required). Since 2026-09-20 the gate is a sanity filter (cost
drag <= 15%/yr, <= 1 fill per pair per day, >= 30 trades, drawdown no worse
than the larger of 30% and 0.75x the basket's own) and does not require in
sample profit, so "failed the old gate" is not by itself a reason to skip an
idea. The mechanism critiques still stand where they are given (for example
"long the wrong direction more than the wrong pairs"); the gate numbers do
not, and should be rerun with `--gate` before an idea here is treated as
closed. The 2026-09-20 entries (rebalancing, volume breakout, breadth) were
judged on their own arithmetic, not the gate, and are unaffected.

- Anything that trades more than a few times per pair per day. P1.
- Parameter nudges of a few percent to the champion. They cannot be told apart from noise in a 21 day window, so they can only waste the slot.
- Regime filter for the momentum champion (vol or EMA gated entries): tried four ways against the real trailing 365 day window (2026-09-17/18, all ten pairs, live cost model) — a 500h realised-vol-median gate (lost 42.6%, DD -58.7%, cost coverage -0.99), a 672h EMA regime gate (lost 50.0%, DD -61.3%, cost coverage -1.72), and (as a z-score stop-loss variant on mean_reversion rather than an entry filter) a loose stop at 240/2.0/0.5/2.0 (cost coverage -1.01) and a tight stop with a rarer entry at 240/3.5/1.0/1.0 (cut drawdown to -20.0% but cost coverage -2.24, gross pnl still negative). None came close to backtest_gate's bar (cost_coverage >= 1.0, max_drawdown >= -30%). Correction to the version of this idea originally logged here: it is not blocked by candle history (the two year backfill has been present since 2026-09-17) — `bot/run.py` and `bot/backtest.py` both cap every strategy call to the trailing `risk.yaml:history_hours` (720 hours / 30 days at the time; raised by Fin to 2160 hours / 90 days on 2026-09-20) regardless of how much history exists, on purpose, so live paper trading can never be kinder than the backtest. A regime filter longer than ~29 days was not implementable then; up to ~90 days is now, and the versions that are implementable (500-672h, close to the ceiling) have now been tried and failed. Over the trailing year every one of the ten pairs fell 46-81% peak to trough (see notes/2026-09-19.md); with no shorting, a filter that just thins out how often a long-only signal fires does not fix a mechanism that is net long during a sustained one-directional fall. Not worth another attempt at "filter momentum's or mean reversion's entries" without a mechanism that is flat by construction during that kind of move, not just a tighter trigger on the same signal.
- Cross sectional selection (hold only the top N pairs by relative strength; SUPERSEDED IN PART 2026-10-05: the weekly rebalanced version is reopened under Candidates, what follows stands for hourly re-ranking only): tried two ways against the real trailing 365 day window (2026-09-17) — a standalone relative-strength ranker (top 3 of 10 by trailing 168h return) churned up to 10 fills/pair/day (over the gate's 4/day limit) and lost money outright (cost coverage -0.63 to -2.45) across several settings; an overlay that keeps ts_momentum's own entry/exit bands but caps concurrent positions to the top 3 qualifying pairs by signal strength didn't churn (0.64 trades/pair/day) but still lost 77.8% with an 81.7% drawdown, worse than plain ts_momentum's own 71.0%/77.9% over the same window. Concentrating capital into fewer positions doesn't help when the whole universe is falling together — both versions were long the wrong direction more than they were long the wrong pairs. Not worth retrying without a mechanism for calling the direction, not just the relative ranking.
- Equal-weight rebalancing across the basket (volatility harvesting / "Shannon's demon" premium, no directional signal at all): computed directly from state/history (2026-09-20) — average pairwise return correlation across the ten pairs is ~0.79 (trailing 90d and trailing 365d agree), which caps the mechanical rebalancing bonus (0.5 x (average variance - basket variance), annualised) at only ~3.7-4.8% of equity before costs. Too thin to clear a 30 bps round trip once spread across the turnover needed to harvest it, and it is not flat during a one-directional fall the way H2's mechanism is: a fully invested rebalanced basket still tracks close to the basket's own ~53% trailing-year drawdown, since the bonus only shaves a little off the top. Not worth building.
- Volume-confirmed breakout (new N-hour high plus trailing volume elevated vs its own longer baseline, exit on a trailing low breakdown): simulated against the real trailing 365 days with entry/exit hysteresis matching how strategies here actually hold positions (120h high, volume 72h average > 1.5x its own 720h baseline, exit below a 72h low). 24 trades across the ten pairs, average -4.36% per trade — a volume surge did not distinguish genuine breakouts from the false starts that get chopped up in a sustained fall. Same failure mode as the already-killed breakout/momentum variants; the extra precondition didn't help. Not worth building.
- Cross-sectional breadth gate (trade only when a fraction of the ten pairs are above their own N-hour EMA, a market-wide risk-on confirmation rather than a pair's own regime): checked directly whether breadth level predicts the basket's own forward 24h return over the trailing 365 days (2026-09-20) — it does not; average forward return is indistinguishable across low/mid/high breadth terciles, all within noise of zero. Breadth describes what already happened, not what happens next, so a gate built on it has no more timing power than the already-killed per-pair EMA/vol regime filters. Not worth building.
