## Candidates

- Slow trend, the champion's own signal at ten times the horizon (added 2026-09-25 by Fin's session; strongest lead in sample so far). ts_momentum with lookback_hours 720 (30 days), ema_hours 168, entry_return 0.05, exit_return -0.05, ema_exit_buffer 0.05, same vol scaled sizing as H0. Real backtest engine: last 365 days -13.5% while the basket fell 50.1% (max drawdown -45%, 398 fills, 0.11 per pair per day, cost drag 5.6%/yr, skill vs exposure matched benchmark +10.3%, gate PASS); last 700 days +35.0% while the basket finished +1.4% (Sharpe 0.58, cost drag 14.3%/yr, max drawdown -56.8%). Quarters over 700 days, strategy/basket: +34.5%/+33.7%, +11.0%/+48.6%, -26.4%/-58.5%, +23.6%/+23.7%: it keeps most of the up moves and about half of the down moves, which is what trend following is supposed to do. Why it should work where H0 does not: in a crude screen of the same signal at three horizons (fixed 10% per pair, 15 bps a side), the mean gross move per round trip was +4 bps at 72h (a coin flip against a 30 bps round trip, 2,069 round trips), +58 bps at 168h (743) and +294 bps at 720h (250). Cautions: one parameter set picked after looking at three horizons on this data, and two years hold only one bull and one bear leg. Its edge shows up over months, not weeks: on the usual exposure skill basis its 60 day skill was positive in only 54% of historical windows (mean +0.1%), because most of the value came from sitting out one crash. A 60 day window will mostly read it as noise; say so in the ledger entry and read its verdict accordingly.
- Longer horizon: 168 hour lookback with wider bands (entry +3%, exit −3%). Fewer, bigger moves; the cost per unit of captured move falls roughly in proportion. Tried against the current data (2026-09-16): backtest cost coverage was worse than H0's, not better (-4.75 vs -0.96) — wider bands wait longer to exit, which let losses run further in this falling window. The "fewer, bigger moves" logic held for entries but not for the matching exit; a version of this idea should pair the wider entry band with a faster exit, not the same one.
- Weekend throttle: no new entries from Friday 20:00 UTC to Monday 00:00 UTC. Liquidity thins and spreads widen at the weekend, so the modelled 5 bps slippage understates the true cost of those fills.
- Volatility targeted exit: exit when a position's trailing 24h move against it exceeds 1.5 times its hourly vol times sqrt(24). A stop expressed in vol units rather than a fixed return, so it adapts per pair. Tried as an add-on to ts_momentum's exit against the current data (2026-09-16): still failed the backtest cost coverage gate (-0.89, vs H0's own -0.96), a small improvement but not enough. Worth retrying once a challenger has actually run so there is live decisions.csv evidence of how ts_momentum's exit lags, rather than backtest-only.

- Anything that trades more than a few times per pair per day. P1.
- Parameter nudges of a few percent to the champion. They cannot be told apart from noise in a 21 day window, so they can only waste the slot.
- Regime filter for the momentum champion (vol or EMA gated entries): tried four ways against the real trailing 365 day window (2026-09-17/18, all ten pairs, live cost model) — a 500h realised-vol-median gate (lost 42.6%, DD -58.7%, cost coverage -0.99), a 672h EMA regime gate (lost 50.0%, DD -61.3%, cost coverage -1.72), and (as a z-score stop-loss variant on mean_reversion rather than an entry filter) a loose stop at 240/2.0/0.5/2.0 (cost coverage -1.01) and a tight stop with a rarer entry at 240/3.5/1.0/1.0 (cut drawdown to -20.0% but cost coverage -2.24, gross pnl still negative). None came close to backtest_gate's bar (cost_coverage >= 1.0, max_drawdown >= -30%). Correction to the version of this idea originally logged here: it is not blocked by candle history (the two year backfill has been present since 2026-09-17) — `bot/run.py` and `bot/backtest.py` both cap every strategy call to the trailing `risk.yaml:history_hours` (720 hours / 30 days at the time; raised by Fin to 2160 hours / 90 days on 2026-09-20) regardless of how much history exists, on purpose, so live paper trading can never be kinder than the backtest. A regime filter longer than ~29 days was not implementable then; up to ~90 days is now, and the versions that are implementable (500-672h, close to the ceiling) have now been tried and failed. Over the trailing year every one of the ten pairs fell 46-81% peak to trough (see notes/2026-09-19.md); with no shorting, a filter that just thins out how often a long-only signal fires does not fix a mechanism that is net long during a sustained one-directional fall. Not worth another attempt at "filter momentum's or mean reversion's entries" without a mechanism that is flat by construction during that kind of move, not just a tighter trigger on the same signal.
- Cross sectional selection (hold only the top N pairs by relative strength): tried two ways against the real trailing 365 day window (2026-09-17) — a standalone relative-strength ranker (top 3 of 10 by trailing 168h return) churned up to 10 fills/pair/day (over the gate's 4/day limit) and lost money outright (cost coverage -0.63 to -2.45) across several settings; an overlay that keeps ts_momentum's own entry/exit bands but caps concurrent positions to the top 3 qualifying pairs by signal strength didn't churn (0.64 trades/pair/day) but still lost 77.8% with an 81.7% drawdown, worse than plain ts_momentum's own 71.0%/77.9% over the same window. Concentrating capital into fewer positions doesn't help when the whole universe is falling together — both versions were long the wrong direction more than they were long the wrong pairs. Not worth retrying without a mechanism for calling the direction, not just the relative ranking.
- Equal-weight rebalancing across the basket (volatility harvesting / "Shannon's demon" premium, no directional signal at all): computed directly from state/history (2026-09-20) — average pairwise return correlation across the ten pairs is ~0.79 (trailing 90d and trailing 365d agree), which caps the mechanical rebalancing bonus (0.5 x (average variance - basket variance), annualised) at only ~3.7-4.8% of equity before costs. Too thin to clear a 30 bps round trip once spread across the turnover needed to harvest it, and it is not flat during a one-directional fall the way H2's mechanism is: a fully invested rebalanced basket still tracks close to the basket's own ~53% trailing-year drawdown, since the bonus only shaves a little off the top. Not worth building.
- Volume-confirmed breakout (new N-hour high plus trailing volume elevated vs its own longer baseline, exit on a trailing low breakdown): simulated against the real trailing 365 days with entry/exit hysteresis matching how strategies here actually hold positions (120h high, volume 72h average > 1.5x its own 720h baseline, exit below a 72h low). 24 trades across the ten pairs, average -4.36% per trade — a volume surge did not distinguish genuine breakouts from the false starts that get chopped up in a sustained fall. Same failure mode as the already-killed breakout/momentum variants; the extra precondition didn't help. Not worth building.
- Cross-sectional breadth gate (trade only when a fraction of the ten pairs are above their own N-hour EMA, a market-wide risk-on confirmation rather than a pair's own regime): checked directly whether breadth level predicts the basket's own forward 24h return over the trailing 365 days (2026-09-20) — it does not; average forward return is indistinguishable across low/mid/high breadth terciles, all within noise of zero. Breadth describes what already happened, not what happens next, so a gate built on it has no more timing power than the already-killed per-pair EMA/vol regime filters. Not worth building.
